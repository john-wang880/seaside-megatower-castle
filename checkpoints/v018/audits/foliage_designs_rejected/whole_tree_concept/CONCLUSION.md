# Outcome and useful art-direction conclusion

**Owner rejected this isolated whole-tree concept. Retain accepted v018 unchanged.** Front/side silhouettes show stacked bracket-like blocks around a central spike; neutral oblique confirms large upright slab walls. The new structure is different from the old pears but not a convincing interpretation of radial conifer boughs. No full-scene integration is justified. No parameter tuning follows.

This closes only the tested recipes, not the possibility of a good foreground tree. The original close-view spindle weakness remains real. The sparse spray experiment lost canopy mass; the dense three-lobe experiment retained bulk but retained pear organization; this whole-crown fan experiment replaced that organization with overly rectilinear shelf walls. Passing opacity, envelope or source-preservation constraints cannot substitute for a convincing primary shape.

The useful direction is still **dense, irregular, outward branching canopy hierarchy with tapered tips and downward secondary masses**, at the scene's narrow crown proportions. These words do not mean flat radial disks, constant-thickness fan walls, stacked collars, or literal needles. A future attempt would need a genuinely resolved three-dimensional branch-mass shape and accepted isolated silhouette before procedural repetition or scene integration—not another numerical tweak to these failed primitives. That is an asset-design problem; no claim is made that the wider project or all safe modeling scope is exhausted.

There is no reason to ask the user to choose among the failed recipes or to portray the original style as a defect they must resolve. Existing stylized foliage remains the better tested scene asset. Parent can defer foliage while judging other substantial, evidence-supported development, or seek a specific visual reference/direction if a future foliage style change materially departs from the current design. No new rooms, surface-noise chores, or arbitrary checkpoints are implied.
